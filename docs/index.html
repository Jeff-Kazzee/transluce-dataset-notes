Notes on Transluce's urlquery agent-activity dataset

Transluce published its report on 2026-09-23 and asked readers to keep digging into the data. Here are three things we found in its public files. Every number comes from one script you can run yourself.

1. Agents chased a US federal budget report through urlquery 586 times in four days

Source: Transluce's public dataset of urlquery.net reports.

586reports in the MAX budget documents bucket, May 24 to 27, 2026
MAX.govthe Office of Management and Budget (OMB) portal
0mentions of MAX.gov or this count in the article

Transluce labels these 586 as source requests (267), relay or intermediary use (318), and one custom program. None of their reason texts mentions a probe or exploit. As far as the labels show, this was retrieval, not a hack.

How it ties to OpenAI, in Transluce's own words. The article says the wiki "links directly to urlquery.net exactly once, on May 26, pointing to a scan of a federal budget data PDF" and that "this PDF is discussed by wiki agents in dozens of other pages." It describes the DseWiki traffic as something "which OpenAI has publicly acknowledged as originating from them." The dataset's methods.json calls the document the "Exact document linked from wiki to a URLQuery report."

What this supports: the urlquery activity chased the same federal document that the OpenAI-acknowledged wiki agents were working on. What it does not prove: that every one of the 586 scans came from OpenAI. Transluce writes that "it seems like the individual agents involved were largely distinct" between urlquery and the wiki.

2. Nearly a quarter of the included reports come from an outside catalog

8,968included reports marked as members of an "externally selected research-activity cohort"
24%of the 37,649 included reports
229of them included despite reason text that says "needs review"

The dataset's methods.json names the source: a "research activity explorer" at research-activity-september-2026.tuxianeer.chatgpt.site, dated 2026-09-21. All 8,968 reviewed urlquery rows in that explorer appear in the dataset. The article text never mentions the explorer. The credit is in the data files.

Every significant row that cites the cohort also lists other evidence. So this does not show that the significant set rests on the explorer alone. It does mean the dataset and the explorer are not independent sources.

3. The activity ran 57 days without a break

57consecutive UTC days with 10 or more included reports
Apr 26 to Jun 21then zero on June 22
Sundaythe most active weekday since March 1. No weekend dip.

The article describes a May and June peak that collapses on June 22. The unbroken run and its start date are not in the text.

Open questions, not findings

Some reports on the same tasks may sit outside the dataset because the search patterns miss domain variants. For example, the pattern *whssgr.com* in methods.json does not match whssgrgupkar.com. Our counts for these come from urlquery search results and are not yet verified record by record.

Reproduce

python scripts/verify_claims.py \
  --dataset data/urlquery-agent-activity-2026-09-22-v5 \
  --page data/agent-activity.txt \
  --explorer data/tux_data.json

Download the dataset from Transluce's page and save the article text first. results/claims.json holds our output with SHA-256 hashes of every input.

"Not in the article" means no match in our copy of the page text, saved 2026-09-25. We searched news coverage, Hacker News, and the web that day and found no prior write-up of these points.
